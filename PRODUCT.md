# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Ayşenur (team leader), designers (Aylin, Mert, etc.), and Oktay (printer) at Yükselen Zeka. They use this daily during work hours, on desktop, to track and manage book publishing projects through a multi-stage pipeline. Ayşenur is the primary power user — she creates projects, assigns them, and approves/rejects at each stage.

## Product Purpose

YZ Yayın Takip replaces spreadsheet/analog workflows with a unified publishing pipeline tracker. Every book project (TR or ÇİN type) moves through defined stages from design to sale. Success means Ayşenur can see every project's status at a glance, designers know exactly what to do next, and no stage transition is missed or undocumented.

## Brand Personality

Warm, capable, organized. The tool is made by and for a small, close-knit team — it should feel like their space, not a generic SaaS product. Approachable and slightly feminine (the team skews female), but never precious or distracting from the work.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS gray (Notion, Linear) — cold, impersonal, interchangeable
- Social/consumer app casualness (Instagram, Pinterest) — too playful, not a work tool
- Heavy enterprise density (Jira, SAP) — overwhelming, discouraging
- AI-default warm cream/sand backgrounds — not differentiated, generic

## Design Principles

1. **Status at a glance** — any user should understand every project's state within 2 seconds of landing on the screen.
2. **Role clarity** — the UI surfaces only what each role can act on; irrelevant actions stay invisible.
3. **Warmth without distraction** — the rose/pink identity is an asset, not decoration; it should reinforce the team's ownership of the tool without slowing them down.
4. **Pipeline trust** — stage transitions, rejections, and history must feel deliberate and traceable, not casual.
5. **Small team scale** — this is not an enterprise product; don't add complexity that serves nobody in this org.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Turkish locale throughout. Desktop-first (team uses this at their desks), but should not break on smaller screens.
